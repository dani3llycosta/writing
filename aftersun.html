Aftersun — Movie Review

“When you were eleven, what did you think you would be doing now?” — Aftersun (2022)

Written by Danielly Costa • Nov 2025
Content Warning: The movie deals with themes that may be triggering for some viewers, such as mental health and depression. If you are sensitive to certain topics, please take a moment to look into the trigger warnings related to the film before watching.

Synopsis

Sophie reflects on the bittersweet memories of a holiday she took with her father twenty years earlier. Memories fill in the gaps between recordings they made as she tries to come to terms with the version of her father she knew and the turbulent parts of him she never got to see.

How the story is told

Aftersun makes you reflect on themes like memory, grief, and what it means to be human. It is a deeply thoughtful film; the crew did a wonderful job placing the audience right beside the characters, creating a very intimate atmosphere where you often feel like you shouldn’t even be “present” in that moment.

What makes the plot work

Charlotte Wells’s writing has a mind of its own. She allows the viewer to connect the dots and draw their own conclusions about what is really happening beneath the surface, contributing to an atmosphere that is almost dreamy. We are taken on a journey through Sophie’s memories and her emotionally charged reflections, and we end up connecting with both protagonists.

How much of our parents do we truly know? And when do we start realizing they are just as human as we are?

How the film looks

The cinematography is one of the film’s strongest elements for me. From the colors and lighting to the composition of each scene, the movie builds its audiovisual narrative beautifully, establishing the mood and the vision being conveyed while guiding the viewer’s gaze.

My experience

The film carries a tangible softness. I saw myself in both Sophie and Calum. The feeling of not belonging anywhere, the emotions we cannot always understand, the attempt to find your place in the world.

Through Sophie’s experiences, I revisited so many emotions I felt at her age. And through Calum’s quiet melancholy as he navigates feelings and situations that are familiar to many of us, I found myself reflecting on life itself.

As a child, I believed that being an adult meant knowing exactly where you were going. I imagine Sophie thought the same about her father, and maybe he also believed that at some point in his life.

When do we truly grow up?

Is it worth watching?

Yes, if you enjoy emotionally charged, multilayered films, you’ll love it. For those who aren’t as familiar or comfortable with a slower narrative pace, I still think it’s worth stepping out of your comfort zone; you might be surprised. I personally recommend it a lot. It was one of the films that marked me the most this year and has the potential to offer a new perspective on life.

Overall rating

5 / 5
